Seven Steps to Win a ₹10 Lakh Work Order (SEEID Maharashtra)

STEP 1 • IDENTIFY THE PROBLEM

Who loses what: Mumbai dumps 6,500 MT/day refuse. Malad/Borivali mandi vendors lose 12–15 kg unsold veggies daily because of no cold storage, creating open rotting, hazardous pests, and toxic leachate.

STEP 2 • ONE-LINE SOLUTION

One clear line: A modular 10x10 sq ft on-site mini digester that mechanically sorts and converts market waste into 5 kW power and organic manure so that landfill transportation stops.

STEP 3 • METHOD & HONEST FAILURE

The failure & fix: Our first rough prototype trommel mesh clogged violently within 40 minutes when handling fibrous banana stems and cornhusks. We retrofitted high-shear counter-rotating cutter knives and water-jet assist.

STEP 4 • NUMBERS-BASED IMPACT

Before vs After: Conventional BMC waste disposal costs ₹2,500+ per ton in logistics and tipping. Our localized processing costs ₹450/tonne, slashing ward sanitation expenditure by 75% while diverting 350+ MT/year.

STEP 5 • GOVERNMENT OWNER

Client department: Chief Engineer (SWM), Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC), Assistant Municipal Commissioner (P/North & R/Central Ward), and MSInS Maharashtra.

STEP 6 • SCALE IN CIRCLES

Expansion curve: 1 site (Malad Mandi 10x10 platform) → 5–10 sites (Western Suburbs Mandis) → All 24 BMC Municipal Wards → MMRDA / TMC / NMMC.

Real-World Industrial Benchmark: Banas–Suzuki (Agthala, Gujarat)

INSPIRE 2026 BENCHMARK
BANAS-SUZUKI BENCHMARK
100 TPD Dung → 1.5 TPD CBG

A landmark project demonstrating large-scale cow-dung-to-CBG production, proving biological waste-to-energy viability in India.

OUR DIFFERENTIATION (THE REAL ENGINEERS)
Municipal MRF + Biogas + Power

While Banas-Suzuki relies solely on cow dung for CBG, our system incorporates mechanical municipal waste sorting to diversify feedstock, producing both clean electricity and Bio-CNG.

“Separate First, Recover Next” — 4-Stream MRF Cascade
1. Mechanical Sorting:
Trommel screen (size separation), Magnetic separator (ferrous metals), Air classifier (light plastics).
2. Recyclables to Industry:
Clean PET bottles, aluminium cans, and glass diverted straight to authorized recycling aggregators.
3. Refuse Derived Fuel (RDF):
Non-recyclable combustible dry fraction baled and supplied as RDF to cement kilns for co-processing.
4. Clean Wet Organics:
Vegetable refuse enters our anaerobic digester for methane and FCO bio-manure production.

Two Energy Pathways: Flexible Dispatch Architecture

OPTION 01: ELECTRICITY GENERATION (CHP)

Cleaned biogas fuels a Combined Heat & Power (CHP) / Dual-Fuel engine to produce decentralized electrical power and recover jacket heat locally.

Best For: Decentralized night market power, civic streetlights (35-40 LED lamps), EV compactor charging posts, and digester 37°C mesophilic heating.
OPTION 02: COMPRESSED BIO-GAS (CBG)

Upgrading and compressing gas to 200–250 bar to produce Bio-CNG (CBG) — a direct green alternative to fossil CNG for vehicles.

Best For: SATAT scheme fuel, BMC garbage truck fueling, displacement of fossil diesel, and gas-grid injection (PNGRB 2026 compliance).
